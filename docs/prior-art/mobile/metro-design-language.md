## What it is
Microsoft's typographic design language, grown in Windows Media Center and Zune and named "Metro" with Windows Phone 7 (2010). Principles: content before chrome, "authentically digital" (no skeuomorphism), type in Segoe as the main visual element, and motion that links screens. **Panorama** was a wide canvas bigger than the screen with an oversized title that scrolled in parallax; **Pivot** was a row of big tab headers you swiped between.

## What was new
- Hierarchy by type size and weight alone, years before flat design spread (iOS 7, 2013).
- **Parallax titles** that hint there is more to the side, and motion that "turns" pages like a turnstile, giving each transition a direction.
- Clipped text at the screen edge as an affordance for "keep swiping".

## What went wrong / limits
- Legibility and discoverability suffered: clipped titles and text-only buttons made it hard to know what was tappable.
- Applied to the Windows 8 desktop it clashed with mouse and keyboard work.
- Microsoft replaced it with Fluent (2017), which brought back depth, light and material (acrylic).

## Lessons for swaypplet
- **Take type-first hierarchy**: design-system principle 3 is Metro's core, and it is the part that aged well.
- **Avoid flat to the point of no affordance**: Metro's move back to Fluent's material shows that depth cues, which swaypplet's glass gives, carry information.
