## What it is
The Nokia N9 (announced June 2011) ran MeeGo 1.2 Harmattan, a Linux and Qt system with no home button. A swipe starting *off-screen* from any edge left the current app and returned to Home, which was three side-by-side views: **Events** (notifications and feeds), **Applications** (the launcher) and **Open applications** (live thumbnails). An option let a swipe down close the app.

## What was new
- **One gesture from every edge means "out"**, starting on the bezel so it never collides with in-app swipes.
- **Home as three peers** (what happened, what I can start, what is running) instead of one launcher with extras.
- Double-tap to wake onto a lock screen showing time and notifications.

## What went wrong / limits
- Nokia had already chosen Windows Phone (February 2011) before the N9 shipped, so it was a dead end on arrival despite strong reviews.
- The design lived on in Sailfish OS (see [sailfish-covers-pulley](sailfish-covers-pulley.md)), built by former Nokia staff at Jolla.

## Lessons for swaypplet
- **Take the three-view home as a model**: events (notification centre), apps (launcher), running (Super+Tab) are exactly swaypplet's three overlays; making them neighbours reachable from each other with one key is the N9 lesson.
- A product can be killed by strategy with nothing wrong in the UI; judge ideas apart from their fate.
