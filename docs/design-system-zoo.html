5 · Composition

The quick-settings panel, built only from the parts

The panel the Helm shows today, assembled from the components above and the rules next to it.

  1. One glass layerThe panel is the only glass. Tiles, rows and the section header are fills on it.
  2. Accent means on, and the one primary actionThe tiles that are on, the slider fill, and "Lock". Nothing else is accent-coloured.
  3. Sliders own a full rowThey never share a line with a tile or a label that competes with them.
  4. Three text levelsFg for names, muted for state, faint for metadata. Size and weight say importance before colour does.
  5. Status colour for status onlyGreen here means the backup is current. Green is never decoration.
  6. Group with fills, not linesNo dividers between blocks; the gaps and fills do it.

7 · Light, dark and the glass

A mode is a token set and a material tuning, never a new shape

The card keeps painting the same key colour in both modes, only so the compositor can see its shape; the compositor drops those pixels. What the glass shows is the material's own body fill, and that is what a mode changes. So a mode switch rewrites tokens.css and sends five material values to sway, and the mask, the fill key and the cross-repo guard's key check stay exactly as they are.

Material by mode

Everything not listed (refraction, dispersion, bevel, crest, specular, grain) is the same material in both modes. The glass should look like one object that changed its tint, not two products.

How a switch happens

  1. Source of the modeAuto follows the desktop's colour scheme (org.freedesktop.appearance color-scheme through the portal), which GTK 4.20+ already maps to prefers-color-scheme. The night-light schedule can set that scheme at sunset and sunrise, so one clock drives both.
  2. What swaypplet sendsIt regenerates tokens.css for the mode and reloads its one provider, then sends the material values per glass namespace over IPC. That is the path the glass pane's replay already takes; nothing restarts.
  3. WhenManual switches apply at once. Auto waits for the next idle or unlock, so a sunset never flips a panel in front of someone reading it.
  4. What never changesThe fill key (#32302f at 0.50), the mask threshold (0.40), the geometry, and the lock screen's scrim arithmetic. The guard keeps checking them as it does today.
  5. New in the shaderOne sign: a negative photochromic becomes a lift (a floor on luminance) instead of a ceiling. Dark glass caps a bright backdrop; light glass raises a dark one. Everything else exists already.
  6. Nix sideglass.nix keeps one material and adds modes.dark and modes.light overrides for the five values; theme.json carries the mode. The guard checks both overrides against the shipped presets.
  7. Surfaces that ignore the modeNone by default. The lock screen and greeter follow it too; their scrim stays black at 0.20 in both, because it dims the session behind, not the glass.
8 · Contrast, measured through the material

Worst case: white, mid-grey and black behind the glass

APCA Lc of each text token over the glass body, computed with the shader's own steps for the current mode: absorption, the photochromic ceiling or lift, then the body fill. The flat interior of a card is assumed (full path, no lens). Switch Mode and Contrast and watch the fails move.
